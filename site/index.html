The world

Every business matters, because someone is waiting on it.

There is no combat here. The challenge is scarcity, specialisation, shifting demand and the fact that your greenhouse cannot run without somebody else's water.

01

Every business matters

Each one produces something other players or simulated residents genuinely need. Nothing you make is decoration.

02

Easy in, deep to master

A session runs ten to twenty minutes. Pricing, layout, contracts, quality and logistics keep going for months.

03

The world shows its makers

Shops, signs, products and community projects visibly transform each district. The skyline is a record of who built it.

04

Cooperation pays, never forced

Municipal wholesalers stop deadlocks, but a player supplier will always beat them on price, quality and reliability.

05

Earn by contributing

Real earning comes from things other players choose to buy — not from holding land, and not from holding a token.

06

Distance is real

Regional markets, ferries and freight mean a crate has to physically get somewhere. That gap is somebody's whole business.

A session

Ten minutes, start to profit.

Long enough to matter, short enough for a lunch break. Production runs on a clock, so the world keeps working while you're away.

  1. Read the streetCheck stock, open orders, what the neighbourhood is short of, and who's been trading.
  2. Buy your inputsFrom another player if you can, from the expensive municipal supplier if you must.
  3. Set the floor runningConfigure recipes, capacity and schedules, then start the batch.
  4. Keep the machines honestEquipment wears with every job. Maintenance costs coin, parts and somebody's labour.
  5. Sell, serve, deliverThrough your shop, a standing contract, a delivery run or a public event.
  6. ReinvestInto the business, the property, and the neighbourhood project everyone benefits from.

The honest part

What we will and won't do with the economy.

Markets & Makers is being built to become a play-to-earn game on Solana. That makes the rules below more important than the features above.

✓
Earning follows contribution

Rewards come from what other players choose to buy from you, and from work the world actually needed doing.

✗
No passive yield, ever

Holding land, a licence or a token earns nothing on its own. There is no staking dressed up as a business.

✓
A budgeted reward pool

Emission is a fixed schedule shared out by contribution — so the pool cannot be drained by whoever grinds hardest.

✗
No promises of profit

No projected returns, no guaranteed demand, no talk of price. It's a game, and it has to be fun without the token.

✓
The server owns the ledger

Currency, inventory, land and production settle on an audited double-entry ledger, not in your browser.

✗
Land is never an investment product

Plots, utilities and progression are gameplay. They are not sold as assets that pay you.
